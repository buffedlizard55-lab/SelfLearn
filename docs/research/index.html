GEMS Prize research & knowledge base

Research library for the Geologic Enhanced Mapping System (GEMS) Prize (DrivenData competition 306, sponsored by the DOE Office of Geothermal with the National Lab of the Rockies): identifying faults absent from the existing USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue in the GeoDAWN area. This section documents and reasons; it does not generate, validate, or submit predictions — those stay behind an explicit, separate gate. One registered entity, one site: this page lives inside the existing SelfLearn site.

How to read this section. Every research entry carries its source link, what the source establishes (verbatim where quoted), why it matters for this competition, and a confidence note. Verified means: the page was fetched and read on 2026-09-26 (see each anchor's status line), or the number comes from this project's committed, sha-pinned measurement evidence. Anything short of that is labelled. The scoring target is Phase-2-style expert review of new faults, so the catalogue-gap domain is the centrepiece, not a side note.

Research domains

Potential-field geophysics

Magnetic (RTP, TMI, gradients) and isostatic gravity layers, and what gradient-magnitude and tilt-derivative operators imply about buried structural contrasts in this specific survey.

5 research entries

DEM-based structural geomorphology

Curvature and breaks-in-slope for scarp detection; what a subtle or partially buried scarp can look like in this terrain, and what the 1 m DEM adds over the 100 m stack.

4 research entries

Seismotectonics and strain

Strain-rate invariants, earthquake density and conductivity anomalies: where the independent families agree, where they disagree, and what each disagreement means.

3 research entries

Catalogue-gap reasoning

Read the INGENIOUS compilation and the USGS database as methodologies, not just as rasters: how were existing faults actually mapped, and where does that process structurally fail to look?

highest-leverage domain - this is the scored target
